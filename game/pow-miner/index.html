🔧 How the PoW Mini Miner works

What is proof-of-work?

Bitcoin miners compete to find a number (the nonce) that makes the double SHA-256 hash of a block header begin with a long run of zeros. There is no shortcut — the only way is to guess, billions upon billions of times. Every extra zero bit makes a hash twice as rare, so it takes twice the guesses on average.

What your device is doing

  • It builds a real-looking block header on top of the actual latest Bitcoin block (if we can reach mempool.space) and hashes it again and again with a different nonce.
  • It counts the leading zero bits of every result and keeps your best one. Hashes are shown the way Bitcoin shows them, with the zeros up front.
  • It runs the same SHA-256 maths real miners use, in background threads (Web Workers) so the page stays smooth. We checked it against Bitcoin's very first block: it reproduces that hash exactly.

Scoring (in sats)

  • Every time you beat your best, you earn level × 10 sats for each new leading-zero level you reach. Reaching 20 bits for the first time pays 200 sats, 21 pays 210, and so on.
  • Levels get twice as hard each time, so early sats come fast and later ones are rare — just like real mining.
  • Your all-time record is saved on this device. It's play money: no real sats are paid out and nothing is sent to our server.

Power levels

  • Eco uses 1 thread — gentle on batteries. Balanced (default) uses about half your cores. Turbo uses almost all of them: bigger numbers, but warmer and louder.
  • Phones use fewer threads than computers at each level, to protect the battery.
  • Hashing pauses automatically when you switch tabs, and takes a break after 20 minutes to save power. Tap Start to carry on.

Is this crypto-mining?

No — it can't earn you any Bitcoin. It only runs while this game is open, the hashes are worthless, and nothing leaves your device. It's a demonstration of how hard real mining is.

Fun comparison

A single modern mining machine (an ASIC) does around 200 trillion hashes every second — roughly a hundred million times a phone. The whole Bitcoin network is the equivalent of millions of those machines working together. Bitcoin's first-ever block hash had 43 leading zero bits; today's blocks need around 79.
